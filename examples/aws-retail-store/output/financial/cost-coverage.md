## What these figures are, and are not

- Only EC2 instances (aws_instance), EBS volumes (aws_ebs_volume and the root and block devices of an instance), RDS for PostgreSQL instances (aws_db_instance: instance hours, allocated storage, provisioned iops, gp3 throughput), Amazon Aurora (aws_rds_cluster_instance: the instance hours; aws_rds_cluster: the consumed storage and the I/O requests with their price and no quantity) the network families (aws_nat_gateway, aws_lb, aws_eip and the public address an instance asks for, aws_route53_zone, aws_internet_gateway: see the line on the network families below) and the managed-service families (aws_ecs_service on Fargate, aws_mq_broker, aws_elasticache_replication_group and aws_elasticache_cluster, aws_opensearch_domain, aws_dynamodb_table: see the line on them below), the control plane of an Amazon EKS cluster (aws_eks_cluster), the nodes of its managed node groups (aws_eks_node_group) and the storage of S3 buckets and EFS file systems (aws_s3_bucket, aws_efs_file_system: see the line on them below) have lines on the source side; every other AWS source resource has no line and is listed in the out-of-model group.
- The network families have a line on each side, never a total over a partial set. NAT gateway: the hour of a zonal public NAT gateway is priced and the data it processes has the per-GB price and no quantity; the STACKIT price list holds no SKU for a NAT gateway or for outbound address translation, so its target line is PRICE_NOT_FOUND (a service the list does not price is not free). Load balancer: the hour of an Application or Network Load Balancer is priced and its capacity units have the price and no quantity; the target line is the hour of the plan (plan_id; a Network Load Balancer that states none is read as the default p10 the provider documents). Public IPv4 address: the hour of an Elastic IP, and of the address an instance asks for with associate_public_ip_address = true (not an address its subnet or launch template decides), against the hour of a stackit_public_ip. DNS: the month of a hosted zone (the first tier of the Route 53 price; the tier is the account's) and its queries (no quantity); the STACKIT list prices a zone by the number of records it may hold, which the draft does not state, so its target line is AMBIGUOUS. Data transfer out: the per-GB price of the first tier (every tier is named on the line) on the line of the internet gateway and no quantity; the STACKIT list holds no SKU for it, so its target line is PRICE_NOT_FOUND. A quantity that is a measurement of use (GB, capacity unit hours, queries) is never in a Terraform source and is never made up: such a line has its price and no amount. The prices are the On-Demand rows of the AmazonEC2 (NAT gateway, load balancers), AmazonVPC (public IPv4), AWSDataTransfer and AmazonRoute53 offers of the price cache, which `stacktoit prices refresh --provider aws --region R` fetches (`--offer` names one); a line whose offer the cache does not hold names the offer and the command.
- The managed-service families are priced from the On-Demand rows of the AmazonECS (Fargate vCPU and GB hours: the cpu and memory of the task definition times the desired count, which is never taken as zero when it is not stated), AmazonMQ (broker hour by engine, instance type and deployment mode; its storage has the price and no quantity), AmazonElastiCache (node hour by engine and node type times the nodes of a replication group), AmazonES (data and dedicated master node hours, EBS GB-month) and AmazonDynamoDB (provisioned read and write capacity unit hours when the table states them, at the rate beyond the free first tier, which is the whole account's; request units and storage have the price and no quantity) offers, which `stacktoit prices refresh --provider aws --region R` fetches (`--offer` names one); Fargate Spot, ECS on EC2 capacity, Windows tasks, global datastores and non-standard table classes are out of the model. The control plane of an Amazon EKS cluster is the cluster hour of the AmazonEKS offer (the standard-support hour: a cluster of a Kubernetes version past standard support is billed the extended-support hour in addition, which is not in the line, and the line says so; a local cluster on AWS Outposts and a Provisioned Control Plane tier are out of the model; the management fee of EKS Auto Mode is said, not computed). A managed node group costs nothing of its own (Amazon EKS User Guide, Managed node groups): its line is the EC2 hour of the first of its instance_types, or of its launch template, or the t3.medium Amazon EKS uses when none is stated, times its desired_size, with the operating system, tenancy and purchasing option rules of an instance (a Spot or Windows node group is not priced as On-Demand Linux) and the EBS volumes its launch template maps as lines of their own; the root volume of the nodes is not in it. The storage of an S3 bucket (S3 Standard, tiered by the size stored in a month) and of an EFS file system (Standard, or One Zone when availability_zone_name is stated) has its price and no quantity, because the data held is a measurement of use that no Terraform source states: the line names the entry of the assumption file that would price it (variable volume_size, unit GB), and the other storage classes, the requests, the transfer and the throughput are not in it. On the target side a RabbitMQ, Valkey (Key Value Store), OpenSearch or MariaDB instance is the hour of the SKU of its plan_name (the SKU is named by the plan, inferred by StackToIT from the names of the list, and its nodes and disk are checked against the list's attributes); a plan the draft leaves to a person is TARGET_NOT_CHOSEN. An SKE cluster is the cluster hour, and each node pool it states is a line of its own: the server flavor hours of its machine_type (the join of a server) times its nodes, the range between its minimum and its maximum when they differ, an unknown quantity with the price when the draft states either as a variable, and TARGET_NOT_CHOSEN when the machine type is a variable; the boot volume of each node is not in the line, and the machines of a pool are never set against the source lines of the nodes of an EKS node group (a unit of its own) or of an ECS cluster's capacity, so a pool is a target-only line (see below); the cluster hour of an SKE cluster stands against the control plane hour of an EKS cluster, but a unit whose item holds a target-only pool is not computed, so no saving is stated over it. A MongoDB Flex instance is the hour of the SKU MongoDB-Flex-<cpu>.<ram>-<Single\|Replica\|Sharded> of its flavor.cpu, flavor.ram and options.type (inferred by StackToIT from the names of the list, and checked against the SKU's own attributes; the rate of a Replica SKU is read as that of the whole instance with its nodes); a flavor a person sets is TARGET_NOT_CHOSEN, a flavor the list has no SKU of is PRICE_NOT_FOUND, its storage and backups are not computed, and because this build prices no DocumentDB cluster on the source side its line is target only (see below). A software KMS key version and a GB of log ingestion have their list price and no quantity; Secrets Manager is AMBIGUOUS between its tiers (the number of secret versions). A priced target line of a family whose source side has no line for the unit (an SKE cluster and its node pools, a MariaDB instance, a MongoDB Flex instance, a RabbitMQ plan against a source with no priced broker) is target only: it is counted in the STACKIT total, listed apart with its amount (target_only on the line and target_only_lines, target_only_min and target_only_max on the total), and kept out of every comparison and every saving, because no source amount stands against it; each comparison states the lines and the amount it leaves out, on both sides.
- The instance hours and the allocated storage of an RDS for PostgreSQL instance are priced from the AmazonRDS offer of the price cache (On-Demand list prices, Single-AZ or Multi-AZ as the source states; `stacktoit prices refresh --provider aws` fetches it with the AmazonEC2 offer); its provisioned IOPS (io1, io2, gp3) and the provisioned throughput of gp3 storage come from the same offer, gp3 only above the performance its allocated storage includes (a baseline of 12,000 IOPS and 500 MiB/s from 400 GiB, nothing more can be provisioned below it: Amazon RDS User Guide, DB instance storage); without that offer the lines are PRICE_SOURCE_ABSENT and the instance is never in the computed subtotal. An Aurora instance (engine aurora-mysql or aurora-postgresql) is the instance hour of its engine, its instance class and the storage configuration of its cluster, from the same offer (rules version 3 of its narrowing): Aurora Standard when the cluster states no storage_type (the provider's documented default), Aurora I/O-Optimized for storage_type = "aurora-iopt1", and both prices open (AMBIGUOUS) when the source does not state it as a fact or the instance's cluster is not a resource of the source; the cluster's consumed storage (a GB-month) and, on Standard, its I/O requests are measurements of use and have their price and no quantity. An Aurora Serverless v2 instance (db.serverless), an instance of a Multi-AZ DB cluster, backup storage beyond the free allocation, Global Database and the Data API are not computed. An offer narrowed before the Aurora rows were kept answers PRICE_SOURCE_ABSENT with the refresh command on each Aurora line. A PostgreSQL Flex Replica flavor is priced once per instance: the list gives its SKU 3 nodes and a higher rate than the Single SKU (1.6 to 2.0 times it, never 3 times), and the rate is read as that of the whole instance (inferred by StackToIT from the price list; each such line says so); how much of its storage is billed (once or per node) the list does not say, so its storage lines have the SKU and no quantity.
- Source prices are the On-Demand list prices of the AWS offer file in the price cache (USD, net, region of the resource); Spot, Reserved, Savings Plans, Dedicated Host, capacity reservation and private prices are never used. The operating system and licence come from the assumption file: the source does not state them.
- The offer file bills an EBS volume per GB-month; this build reads it as GiB-month, the unit a volume is sized in: the Amazon EBS pricing page states "on this page, GB = 1024^3 bytes" (stated in the documentation for that page; applying it to the offer file's unit is inferred by StackToIT). The IOPS and throughput of a gp3 volume are priced only above the baseline included in the price of its storage (3,000 IOPS, 125 MiB/s; stated in the documentation: Amazon EBS User Guide), per volume; io2 IOPS are priced in tiers whose edges the offer file carries in descriptions only, and are not priced.
- On the target side only STACKIT servers (each with its boot volume), volumes, PostgreSQL Flex instances (flavor, storage class, storage capacity), public IPs, load balancers, DNS zones, RabbitMQ, Valkey, OpenSearch and MariaDB instances (their plan), MongoDB Flex instances (their flavor), SKE clusters (the cluster hour and each node pool), KMS keys, Secrets Manager and Logs instances on the target side; the other drafted resources have a line that says they have no quantity rule.
- The PostgreSQL Flex storage class premium-perf<n>-stackit is priced as the SKU "Block Storage for PostgreSQL Premium-Performance <n>" per disk and its capacity as "Block Storage for PostgreSQL Premium-Capacity" per GB (inferred by StackToIT from the names of the price list; each such line says so). For a replica flavor the list gives the storage per node and does not say whether the booked size is billed once or for each node, so its disks and gigabytes are the range between the two readings. The IOPS and throughput shown for the class in the technical mapping are the attributes maxIOPerSec and maxTroughInMB of that performance SKU in the price list (the catalogue states none for a PostgreSQL Flex class); whether the class covers what the source provisions is a comparison of those figures, not a statement that the two perform alike.
- The lines fall into three groups: the computed subtotal (only the source units every line of which is priced on both sides, so both subtotals cover the same units), the lines whose price is known and whose quantity is not (the quantity that is needed is named), and what is not computed (a unit of a type out of the model, a unit with no line on one side, or a line without a price), listed by resource and never zero.
- Target prices are the net EUR list prices of the public STACKIT price list in the price cache (region eu01). A machine type or performance class the draft leaves to a person is TARGET_NOT_CHOSEN; the metro variant of a SKU is never substituted.
- The target capacity price of a volume is the capacity SKU of its performance class's tier (inferred by StackToIT from the structure of the price list).
- One month is the 730 hours of the scenario; a per-month rate is applied as the file gives it. Snapshots, backups, support plans and every other cost of usage, traffic or requests that this build has no line for are not computed.
- A saving is compared only for the same source units on both sides and in one currency (an exchange rate from the assumption file, with its source and date, converts one side).
- A list price is the price of one unit of one SKU. It is not a cost: this block reads no quantity and builds no total; the quantities, amounts and totals of the priced families are in financial/cost-model.json.
- A resource is joined to a SKU only where the draft itself names the machine type, performance class or flavor; where the draft leaves that to a person (a variable), no SKU is named and the resource is NOT_ASSESSED.
- Prices are net list prices of the public STACKIT price list: excluding taxes, with no contract discount, no savings plan and no other currency. A SKU that is not in the public list, a deprecated SKU and the metro variant of a SKU are never used as a price.
- Egress and traffic, support plans, the container registry and every resource of a kind the price rules do not list have no row: nothing is stated about their cost.
- Only the region eu01 is looked at: it is the region of the knowledge-base catalogue the draft takes its flavors from.
- The row of a volume is the per-hour fee of its performance class; the capacity fee of the disk is a separate SKU that depends on the size, and is a line of the cost model, not a row here.
